# What AI readiness actually means for a small business

"AI readiness" is one of those phrases that gets thrown around at trade shows and in vendor decks without anyone stopping to define it. For a small business owner trying to decide whether to switch on Copilot, hire a "GPT consultant", or just keep ignoring the noise, it can feel useless.

This post is the plain version. What does AI readiness mean for an Irish SME (small or medium-sized enterprise)? What do you need in place before you start, and how do you tell whether your business is actually ready or just hoping for the best?

## What "AI readiness" actually means

AI readiness is not about whether you've signed up for ChatGPT. It's the set of conditions inside your business that decide whether AI tools will be useful, safe, and worth the money — or a leak waiting to happen.

A useful working definition: **a business is AI-ready when it can adopt an AI tool without leaking customer data, breaking a regulation, wasting subscription money, or producing work it has to redo by hand.**

That includes a few things you might not associate with AI at all: who has access to which folders in SharePoint, whether your staff know what they're allowed to paste into a public chatbot, and whether you have one tidy customer list or four conflicting ones across spreadsheets and inboxes.

Readiness is foundational. The AI part is the easy bit — the hard part is the data, permissions and habits underneath it.

## The five things that make a small business AI-ready

When the larger frameworks (NIST, the EU AI Act, the various consultancy models) are stripped down to what an SME actually needs to think about, you end up with five practical pillars.

### 1. Data you can find and trust

AI tools are only as useful as the data they're pointed at. Microsoft 365 Copilot, for example, can summarise a project's emails and documents — but only if those documents actually live in SharePoint or OneDrive in a sensible structure, and only if the right people have access to them.

If your business has files scattered across personal Google accounts, USB sticks, and a shared drive nobody has cleaned up since 2019, an AI assistant will either find nothing or surface the wrong version of the same document.

Ask yourself: if a new staff member needed to find your current price list, terms and conditions, or the latest version of a customer proposal — could they, without asking three people?

### 2. People who know the rules

Your staff are already using AI. They are pasting bits of customer email into ChatGPT to "tidy it up". They are dictating meeting notes into a free transcription tool. They are letting Gmail draft replies for them.

Readiness means they know what's fine, what's risky, and what's off-limits — and that there's a one-page policy somewhere they've actually read. Our [one-page AI policy template](/blog/ai-policy-basics-smes) covers what to include, but the underlying point is simple: the rules need to exist before the incident, not after.

### 3. Processes worth automating

AI is a force multiplier for processes that already work. It will make a tidy quoting workflow faster. It will not fix a chaotic one — it will just produce chaotic output more quickly.

Before you adopt an AI tool, pick one specific process where you can describe, in two or three sentences, what "good" looks like. Customer onboarding emails. Quote follow-ups. Drafting tender responses. Without that clarity, you can't tell whether the AI is helping or just creating new work.

### 4. The right tool, properly licensed

A lot of AI confusion in SMEs comes from mixing up three different things: a free chatbot anyone can use, an AI feature inside a business tool you already pay for, and a tenant-aware enterprise assistant.

The free chatbots (ChatGPT free, Claude free, Gemini free) are fine for generic tasks but should not see customer data. The AI features inside your existing software (Copilot in Microsoft 365, Gemini in Workspace, AI in your accounts package) are usually licensed and governed properly — but you need to switch them on deliberately and price them correctly. Microsoft publishes [official Copilot pricing](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365-copilot/pricing) and the cost adds up quickly across a team.

A common readiness mistake is to buy AI licences for everyone before working out who actually needs them. We cover that in our guide to [reviewing Microsoft 365 licences and permissions](/blog/when-to-review-m365-licences) — the same logic applies before you turn Copilot on.

### 5. Light-touch governance

Governance is a heavy word for what is, for most SMEs, a very small list:

- A short written policy on AI use.
- A nominated person who signs off new AI tools before staff start using them.
- A note in your customer-facing terms (and, where relevant, on chatbots) that AI is being used.
- A periodic check that nothing risky has crept in.

You don't need a formal AI committee. You need someone whose name is next to "AI questions" on the org chart.

## Why this matters now

Two things have changed in the last twelve months that move AI readiness from "nice to have" to "shouldn't ignore".

The first is regulation. The [EU AI Act](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/small-businesses-guide-to-the-ai-act/) is now in force, and from **2 August 2026** the bulk of its obligations apply to all organisations — including SMEs — that use AI systems in the EU. For most small businesses the practical impact is modest: a written policy, transparency about where AI is used (for example, telling customers when they're chatting with a bot), and basic record-keeping. Article 62 explicitly tells regulators to be proportionate to organisation size, and SMEs get priority access to regulatory sandboxes. But "modest" still means you have to actually do it.

The second is competitive. Joint research from [Trinity College Dublin and Microsoft Ireland](https://news.microsoft.com/source/emea/features/trinity-college-dublin-and-microsoft-ireland-research-shows-a-widening-ai-maturity-gap-between-smes-and-large-organisations/) found a widening AI maturity gap between SMEs and larger Irish organisations. Separate reporting around the [AI Works for Ireland](https://carlow.ie/news/2026/80-smes-say-ai-can-transform-their-business-lack-skills-keeps-adoption-rates-low) programme — a Google and Local Enterprise Office partnership offering free regional training — found that around 80% of Irish SME leaders believe AI can transform their business, but skills gaps are holding adoption back.

In other words: the competitors who have done the boring readiness work are pulling ahead, and the ones who haven't are increasingly aware they're behind.

## What "not ready" looks like in practice

If you want a quick gut-check, here are the patterns we see most often in businesses that are not yet ready:

- The owner doesn't know which staff are using AI tools, or for what.
- Customer data is being pasted into free public chatbots.
- Files are spread across personal accounts, local drives and an unloved shared drive.
- Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace permissions haven't been reviewed in over a year.
- Nobody has written down what AI staff can and can't use.
- Copilot or Gemini licences have been bought "just in case" without a defined use case.
- There's no one obvious person to ask about AI questions.

None of these are catastrophic on their own. Together, they mean any AI rollout will produce more risk than benefit.

## A practical first step (it's free)

You don't need to commission a strategy document to find out where you stand. Two free Irish supports cover most of what an SME needs to start.

The **Digital for Business** programme through your [Local Enterprise Office](https://www.localenterprise.ie/growdigital/) gives you a structured assessment of where your business is digitally — including AI and data. It's a prerequisite for the **Grow Digital Voucher**, which then offers up to €5,000 (covering 50% of eligible costs) toward digital tools, training and integration work. Software subscriptions, cybersecurity, configuration and training all qualify.

That voucher is well-suited to AI readiness work — for example, paying half the cost of consolidating your files into SharePoint properly, training staff on a new tool, or hardening your Microsoft 365 setup before turning Copilot on. We've written more about [grants and funding for Irish SMEs](/grants-funding) if you want to see what else might fit.

## Where to go from here

AI readiness is not a one-off project. It's a slightly higher standard for the basics: tidy data, clear permissions, sensible policies, the right licences, and one person who owns the question.

If you do that work first, switching on Copilot or any other AI tool becomes a small, low-stress decision. If you skip it, every AI tool you adopt makes the underlying mess louder.
