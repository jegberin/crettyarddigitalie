# Choosing IT support in Laois, Carlow, and Kilkenny

Picking an IT support provider is one of those decisions small business owners in the Irish midlands and south-east tend to rush. The phone stops working on a Tuesday, someone asks a neighbouring business for a number, and six months later you are locked into a contract you never quite evaluated. The fact that the provider is local is treated as enough.

Local does matter. It is not the whole story. This post is a practical guide to choosing IT support if you are running a small business — an SME (small and medium enterprise) — in Laois, Carlow, or Kilkenny, covering what "local" actually gives you, what the trade-offs are, and how to pick well.

## What "local" genuinely gets you

Three things, when it is real:

**On-site presence when it is needed.** Most IT issues in 2026 get fixed remotely. But the ones that do not — a failed switch, a broken office Wi-Fi, a hardware install, a new staff rollout — are ten times easier when the engineer can be there in 40 minutes rather than by the end of the week.

**Understanding of local context.** An IT provider in Portlaoise, Carlow town, or Kilkenny knows the local broadband providers, the common site conditions, the kinds of businesses the area hosts. They are used to the specific quirks of rural fibre, shared business parks, or listed-building cabling constraints.

**Accountability.** You are not a ticket number in a call centre. You have met the person. If something goes wrong, they are reachable and — importantly — they care, because the next business you talk to might be their own potential client. Reputation in a local market is a powerful alignment mechanism.

## What "local" does not get you

Worth being honest. The fact that a provider is based in Portlaoise, Carlow, or Kilkenny does not automatically mean:

- They are good at what they do.
- Their pricing is fair.
- Their response times are better than a national firm.
- Their security knowledge is current.
- Their contract terms are reasonable.

"We've been doing this for 20 years" is useful context, not a credential. Ask the same questions you would ask a Dublin provider, local or not.

## What to look for

The non-negotiables for any IT support provider, regardless of location:

### 1. Clear scope and written SLA

A written service agreement (often called an SLA — service level agreement) that says plainly:

- What is included in the monthly fee.
- What is extra, and at what rate.
- Response time commitments, by severity.
- How issues are reported.
- What happens in a major incident.
- How contracts end.

If the provider resists putting any of this in writing, that is a warning sign.

### 2. Security as a baseline, not an extra

Good managed IT in 2026 includes MFA (multi-factor authentication — that second code on your phone when you log in), endpoint protection (security software on every laptop and PC), email security tuning, backup testing, and basic user training as part of the package. If those are all "optional extras", you are buying helpdesk, not managed IT. Our [what proactive IT support includes](/blog/what-proactive-it-support-includes) post goes into what to expect.

### 3. Someone who answers the phone

Small business IT support that relies entirely on web forms is a poor fit. You want a human to pick up in a sensible time, especially for urgent issues. Ask how that works in their setup and ask them to describe the last P1 incident (top-priority — everything down) they handled.

### 4. Monthly or quarterly reporting

A written report, in plain English, that shows what was done, what was flagged, what the trends look like. If they do not produce one, they are probably not doing the underlying work.

### 5. Transparent pricing

Indicative per-user per-month figures you can compare. Clear project day-rates. No hidden bolt-ons. Our [pricing](/pricing) page shows how we do this.

### 6. Fair contract terms

Month-to-month or short initial term, reasonable notice period (30-60 days), and a documented offboarding process. Three-year contracts with twelve-month notice are not SME-friendly; the provider should be earning your business every month, not locking you in.

### 7. Local references if you ask

A provider with real Laois, Carlow, or Kilkenny clients should be happy to give you one or two names to call. Not a polished case study — an actual business owner who can tell you what it is like to work with them.

## Questions to ask on the first call

Eight questions that usually sort the serious from the salesy.

1. **What does a typical day look like for your engineers?** Listen for monitoring, patching, proactive work — not just reactive tickets.
2. **Can you send me a sample monthly report?**
3. **How do you handle backups and what is your restore test procedure?**
4. **What is your approach to security for a business my size?**
5. **How do you onboard a new client in the first 30 days?**
6. **What is your escalation path on a P1 incident?**
7. **Where do you physically go on site, and where do you do remote only?**
8. **What is the offboarding process if we ever decide to leave?**

The answers to these are more telling than any marketing site.

## Red flags to walk away from

A short list, in no particular order:

- **Lock-in pressure.** "We can only do this deal if you sign this week."
- **Vague scope.** "We do everything IT" without a written list.
- **No talk of security.** Or talking about it only if you push.
- **Cannot produce a sample report.**
- **Sole operator with no backup plan.** What happens if they are sick, on leave, or unreachable?
- **Hostility to you talking to other providers.** A confident provider is comfortable with you comparing.
- **Poor email hygiene on their own side.** If their own email is a mess, they will not be meticulous with yours.

Any two or three of these in combination, keep looking.
