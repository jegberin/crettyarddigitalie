# When does a small business need managed IT support?

Most Irish SMEs (small and medium enterprises) don't wake up one Monday and decide they need managed IT. It creeps up. A laptop won't join the Wi-Fi, a shared mailbox (an inbox multiple staff can read from, like info@) suddenly won't send, a former employee still has access to SharePoint (the Microsoft file-sharing system your team uses for documents), and nobody can remember who set up the backups — or whether they ever ran. By the time someone asks "should we have a proper IT provider?", the business is already losing hours every week to small problems that compound.

This post is a plain read on when ad-hoc support stops being enough and a managed service starts to earn its keep. No sales pitch — just the signals I see in businesses across Laois, Carlow, and Kilkenny, and what to look for if you decide it's time to make the move.

## What "managed IT" actually means

Before the signals, a quick definition — because the phrase gets thrown around loosely.

**Break/fix** is reactive. Something breaks, you call someone, they fix it, you get an invoice. There is no ongoing responsibility for your environment. The incentive structure quietly rewards problems: the more things go wrong, the more the provider bills.

**Managed IT** is a fixed monthly arrangement where a provider takes ongoing responsibility for your systems. That typically includes monitoring, patching, user account management, backup verification, security controls, and a defined response time when something does go wrong. You're paying for prevention, not firefighting.

The practical difference is that under managed IT, most of the issues that used to turn into tickets simply stop happening, because someone is watching and updating the environment before the user notices. For a plain-language starting point on what good looks like, the [NCSC's SME guidance](https://www.ncsc.gov.ie/guidance/) (the National Cyber Security Centre — Ireland's government cyber agency) is a useful reference.

## Six signs you've outgrown ad-hoc support

### 1. You're the IT department by accident

In a lot of Irish SMEs, "IT" is whichever director is most comfortable with computers. That works for a while, and then it doesn't. The giveaway is when that person is spending real chunks of the working week resetting passwords, troubleshooting printers, or chasing Microsoft licences instead of running the business.

The cost here is hidden. If a managing director bills at €120 an hour and spends four hours a week on IT, that's nearly €25,000 a year of their time going into something a managed provider would cover for a fraction of the cost — and do better.

### 2. Problems keep coming back

Recurring issues are the clearest signal that nobody is fixing root causes. If the same Wi-Fi dead spot has been "fixed" three times, if the same user keeps getting locked out, if Outlook keeps disconnecting on the same laptop — those are symptoms of an environment that nobody owns end-to-end.

Break/fix providers are not paid to find root causes. They're paid to clear the ticket in front of them. A managed arrangement has a direct incentive to stop the same call coming in next month, because the next call eats their margin.

### 3. You don't know the state of your backups

Ask yourself three questions:

- When did your last backup run?
- When was the last time someone restored from it to check it works?
- If the office burned down tonight, what would you lose?

If you can't answer all three confidently, your backups are a hope, not a plan. Backup verification — actually restoring data on a schedule to confirm it works — is one of the least glamorous parts of a managed service and one of the most important. A backup you've never tested is a backup you don't have.

### 4. Staff changes are messy

Onboarding and offboarding are where small businesses quietly leak risk. The pattern is familiar: somebody joins, an email gets set up in a rush, they're given admin rights "just for today", and those rights never come off. Somebody leaves, their mailbox gets forwarded to a colleague, and their Microsoft 365 licence keeps billing for months. Their old OneDrive (the personal cloud storage each Microsoft 365 user gets) is still shared with the client.

A managed provider runs onboarding and offboarding as a defined process: licence assigned or revoked, MFA (multi-factor authentication — that second code on your phone when you log in) enrolled or removed, group memberships updated, mailbox handled per your policy, device wiped or reissued. Nothing is left to memory. If staff turnover is part of your business, this alone can justify the monthly fee — and it's tightly linked to [how your Microsoft 365 tenant is set up](/microsoft-365).

### 5. Security is "we haven't been hit yet"

The NCSC's guidance for Irish SMEs is blunt: a minor incident can have severe impact on a small business. The attackers are not hand-picking you; they're scanning the internet for unpatched software, weak passwords, and mailboxes without multi-factor authentication. You get hit because your front door was the easiest one on the street that morning.

A few questions that usually expose the gap:

- Is MFA enforced for every user, including the owner and the accountant?
- Is legacy email authentication (older sign-in methods like POP/IMAP that skip MFA) blocked?
- Are laptops encrypted and centrally managed, or just "company laptops"?
- Would you know within 24 hours if a staff mailbox was compromised?

If any of those are "I'm not sure", you're carrying risk you haven't priced. Managed IT and [cybersecurity](/cybersecurity) overlap heavily in practice, because the controls that prevent most incidents — patching, MFA, monitoring, backups — are managed-service bread and butter.

### 6. Growth is about to make things worse

Five people sharing a single printer and a consumer router is a workable mess. Fifteen people is not. The transition points I see most often are:

- Adding a second office or a remote/hybrid team
- Hiring past ten employees
- Handling client data that falls under GDPR (the EU data protection rules) duties you can't wing
- Bringing in any software that touches payments or health data
- Moving from a shared inbox to proper shared mailboxes, Teams, and SharePoint

At each of those points, the margin for improvisation drops. You need somebody whose job is to get the Microsoft 365 permissions, the [office network](/network-wifi-security), and the device policy right before growth amplifies the mess.

## What a decent managed plan actually includes

Plans vary, but the baseline for a small Irish business should cover:

- **User and device management** — joiner/leaver processes, device enrolment, patching, encryption
- **Microsoft 365 tenant care** — your tenant is your company's own walled-off area of Microsoft 365; care covers licence reviews, MFA enforcement, sharing and retention policies (rules for how long emails and files are kept), basic audit logging
- **Backup** — including mailboxes and SharePoint/OneDrive, plus scheduled restore tests
- **Security baseline** — endpoint protection (security software on every laptop and PC), legacy auth blocked, Conditional Access (rules that check who, where, and what device before letting someone sign in) where appropriate
- **Monitoring and alerts** — somebody looks at the alerts that matter, and ignores the noise
- **Helpdesk with a real SLA (service level agreement — a written promise of response times)** — a response-time target you can hold them to in writing
- **A named contact** — not a ticket portal in another country with a 24-hour turnaround

If you're being quoted a suspiciously cheap number, the usual missing pieces are backup verification, security baseline work, and joiner/leaver processes. Those are the bits that hurt most when they're absent.

## What it costs in Ireland

Pricing in the Irish SME market is typically per user per month, with a small setup/onboarding fee up front. The exact figure depends on how many users you have, whether laptops are included, and how much of your security baseline already exists.

The comparison that matters isn't managed IT versus doing nothing — it's managed IT versus the true cost of the current mess: director time spent on IT, lost hours when something breaks, emergency call-outs, the occasional disaster, and the very real possibility of a compromised mailbox leaving the business with a bill and a reputation problem. One bad week tends to cost more than a full year of a reasonable managed plan.

## How to choose a provider

A few things to weigh up before you sign anything:

1. **Ask who actually does the work.** Some providers subcontract everything. That's not automatically bad, but you should know.
2. **Read the SLA carefully.** "Best-effort response" is not an SLA. You want response times in hours, not business days, with defined priority levels.
3. **Ask what happens at offboarding — yours.** If you leave, do you get your data, documentation, admin credentials, and tenant ownership back cleanly? The answer should be yes, in writing.
4. **Ask for references in your size range.** A provider who mostly services 200-user clients will often treat a 12-person business as an afterthought.
5. **Look for Irish presence.** Not a brand requirement, but for SMEs with regulatory and practical obligations in Ireland, a provider who understands Revenue, HSE, and GDPR in local context tends to save you time.
