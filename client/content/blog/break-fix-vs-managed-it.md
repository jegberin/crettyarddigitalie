# Break/fix vs managed IT — what actually makes sense for an SME

The question comes up fairly often with small business owners in Ireland. They have been running on break/fix — calling a local technician when something goes wrong and paying for the hour — and they are wondering whether a managed IT contract would actually save money. The honest answer is: it depends on the size of the business, the tolerance for downtime, and a few specific risk factors. This post walks through how to think about it, particularly for SMEs (small and medium enterprises).

## The two models in one paragraph each

**Break/fix** is pay-per-incident. Something stops working, you phone a technician, they drive out or dial in, they fix it, you get an invoice for the time and parts. There is no contract, no ongoing monitoring, and no obligation on either side between calls. For a very small business with simple IT needs, it can be perfectly sensible.

**Managed IT** is a flat monthly fee that covers ongoing monitoring, patching, backups, user support, and a bundle of proactive work. The provider has a continuous relationship with the business, usually with agreed response times and a set amount of support time included. Incremental projects — office moves, new starters, major changes — are quoted separately but at a known rate.

## Where break/fix is genuinely cheaper

Do not let anyone tell you break/fix is always a false economy. For a genuinely small, technology-light business — a sole trader, a two-person office with a laptop each and email in the cloud — break/fix can be the right answer. Three or four incidents a year at €100-200 each can cost less than a full managed contract.

The specific shape where break/fix makes sense:

- Fewer than five users.
- Everything in the cloud — no office server, no on-premises software.
- Low tolerance for occasional short outages.
- No staff whose hourly value makes downtime expensive.
- Nothing regulated or security-sensitive that needs documented controls.

If that is your business, a good local technician on speed-dial is probably fine. What you want from them, even in a break/fix arrangement, is someone who does quality work, responds when you call, and does not charge for the drive each way.

## Where break/fix starts to bite

The crossover happens somewhere around five to ten users, or sooner if the business has specific risk factors. The symptoms:

- **The bill is unpredictable.** One quarter is €400; the next is €2,800 because something broke. Cash flow hates this.
- **Issues pile up because each call costs money.** Staff work around problems rather than report them. Small annoyances become big ones.
- **Nobody is patching.** The technician fixes what breaks; nobody is watching whether Windows updates are applying, whether firewall firmware is current, whether backups are actually running.
- **Security is not proactive.** MFA (multi-factor authentication — that second code on your phone when you log in) never gets rolled out. Staff reuse passwords. The tenant (your company's own walled-off area of Microsoft 365) has never been reviewed. When something does happen, you find out weeks later.
- **The incident response is slow.** You call on Monday morning, the technician is booked until Wednesday, the business loses two days.

Each of those is a cost that does not appear on the break/fix invoice but shows up in lost hours and eventually in a larger incident.

## The costs, honestly

Managed IT contracts in Ireland for small businesses typically run €60-120 per user per month, depending on what is included. The lower end covers support and basic monitoring; the higher end includes full security, backup, device management, and licensing bundled in.

Break/fix charges typically run €75-125 per hour, with minimum billable amounts (usually half an hour or an hour). A typical "something is wrong" call consumes an hour or two.

A simple comparison for a 10-user business:

- **Break/fix at 40 hours a year** (one incident a month, plus a handful of longer ones): €4,000-6,000 plus the cost of downtime.
- **Managed at €80 per user**: €9,600 for the year, but the support hours, monitoring, patching, and some security features are all included.

On face value, managed looks more expensive. The question is what you are comparing. Managed includes the patching, the monitoring, the backups being tested, and the staff training — which on break/fix would be additional line items or, more often, are simply not happening. Costed honestly, the gap narrows or reverses.

## The risk math

The harder-to-cost difference is risk. Break/fix means you are one incident away from a large bill and an outage whose duration depends on how quickly you can reach a technician who happens to be free. A ransomware incident at an SME with no offline backups is often a five-figure event plus a week of downtime. A single business email compromise is usually in the same range.

Managed IT does not eliminate these risks, but it pushes them sharply down. MFA is enforced, backups are tested, email filtering is tuned, staff are trained, and if something does happen, you are on the phone to a provider who knows your environment and has a response plan. The value here is not a line item on the invoice; it is what does not happen.

## A halfway option

Some Irish providers offer a light-touch managed plan for businesses that are not ready for full coverage: a fixed monthly fee that covers monitoring, patching, and a handful of support hours, with everything else billed at an agreed rate. It works well for very small businesses who want the preventive side without the full overhead.

## How to decide

A five-minute test.

1. **How many users and devices do you have?** Under five: break/fix is probably fine. Over ten: managed is almost certainly cheaper once you include everything. In between: depends on the rest of the answers.
2. **How much downtime can your business absorb?** If a day without email costs you nothing: break/fix. If a day without email costs four figures: managed.
3. **Do you have client data or financial data that needs protecting?** If yes, managed is effectively the floor — the controls you need live there.
4. **Is anyone currently doing patching and backup testing?** If the honest answer is no, you have a gap you cannot close on break/fix.
5. **Do you want your IT bill predictable?** If you hate the January surprise invoice, managed wins on that alone.

If the answers point one way clearly, go that way. If they are mixed, a short conversation with a provider who can do either is worth more than more googling.

## What to look for if you do switch

Switching from break/fix to managed is not a big project, but do it deliberately. Ask any prospective provider:

- A sample monthly report.
- Their restore test procedure.
- What happens in the first 30 minutes of a P1 incident (P1 being the top priority — everything down or a serious breach).
- How their contract termination works (month-to-month or annual, notice period, offboarding help).
- What is included, what is extra, written in plain English.

A provider who answers those cleanly is a safe bet. One who hedges is not.

## The quiet case for managed, briefly

If your business depends on its IT working — not just occasionally, but reliably — managed IT is usually the right economic and risk call. Break/fix is a legitimate choice at the very smallest end and a declining one as a business grows.
