# Why SMEs outsource IT — and when it actually makes sense

"We're just going to keep doing it ourselves for now" is a perfectly respectable answer for a small business when it comes to IT. I would never try to talk someone out of it if it is working. The question is whether it is actually working, or whether the business has quietly drifted into a position where the owner is the de-facto IT manager and nobody planned it that way.

This post is about the honest reasons Irish SMEs (small and medium enterprises) eventually outsource IT, the shapes that tend to be a good fit, and how to tell whether you are ready.

## The usual story

Most small businesses do not set out to manage their own IT. It happens gradually. Someone buys the first laptop, someone else figures out Microsoft 365, the owner is "good with computers" so things end up with them. For a while it works. Then the business grows. Staff arrive; questions get asked; the laptop that kept breaking is now three laptops that keep breaking; a phishing email reaches someone; a supplier asks about data protection.

At that point the owner is doing IT support at 9pm on Tuesdays and it is no longer a skill they chose to develop. The question is not whether to outsource; the question is whether the current arrangement is costing more than the alternative.

## The real reasons owners outsource

Stripping away the marketing, three reasons account for most genuine SME moves to outsourced IT.

### 1. The owner's time

The most common, and the most under-stated. An owner who is spending five or ten hours a week on IT issues — support tickets, password resets, setting up new starters, dealing with broken printers — is spending the wrong hours on the wrong problem. Those hours have an opportunity cost that is higher than any IT provider's bill.

The test: what could the owner be doing in those hours? If the honest answer is "growing the business, winning new clients, improving products" then outsourcing the IT is cheaper than keeping it in-house, even if the line-item looks higher.

### 2. Risk exceeds the current coverage

The second reason is the quiet accumulation of risk. A small business that grew from five to twenty staff without an IT plan usually has:

- Inconsistent MFA (multi-factor authentication — that second code on your phone when you log in) coverage.
- Backups that are not tested.
- Staff with more access than they need.
- An unpatched firewall or two.
- Nobody watching for phishing or suspicious logins.

None of this feels urgent day to day. It is the kind of risk that stays invisible until the day it does not. Outsourcing IT to a provider who brings a set of standard baselines — MFA everywhere, tested backups, managed endpoints — is a fast way to close those gaps without hiring an in-house role.

### 3. The business outgrew ad-hoc

At a certain size — usually somewhere between ten and thirty staff — ad-hoc IT stops scaling. New starters are a three-day project. Leavers leave artefacts behind. Everyone has slightly different setups. Small issues recur because nobody has time to actually fix the root cause.

Outsourced IT brings the things ad-hoc cannot: standard builds, documented procedures, monitoring, patching, and a point of accountability. It does not make the business more exciting; it makes it more reliable.

## The cases where in-house is still right

Worth being fair. Outsourced is not always the answer.

**Very small businesses.** Under five users with simple cloud IT and nothing regulated can often manage with a local technician on speed-dial. A full managed contract may be overkill.

**Businesses with significant internal IT expertise.** If you have an in-house IT person who knows your environment and does the job well, outsourcing them away is usually a mistake. Supplementing them with an external partner for specific gaps is the better move.

**Very short-term engagements.** If you are three months away from a sale, merger, or wind-up, a long-term managed contract is usually not the right fit.

Outside these, the case for outsourcing most of the IT function at SME scale gets clearer as the business grows.

## Different flavours of outsourced

"Outsourced IT" is a broad term. The common shapes for Irish SMEs:

**Break/fix with a local technician.** Pay-per-incident. Cheap when nothing breaks. Costly when things do. See our [break/fix vs managed IT](/blog/break-fix-vs-managed-it) post for the comparison.

**Co-managed IT.** Your in-house IT person keeps the relationship with users; the external provider handles infrastructure, security, and after-hours support. A useful hybrid for mid-sized businesses.

**Fully managed IT.** External provider handles everything — helpdesk, infrastructure, security, projects. The right fit for most small businesses without an in-house IT function.

**Project-only.** An external provider handles specific engagements (M365 — Microsoft 365 — migration, network refresh, website build) and you run everything else yourself. Useful when you want help on the big moves only.

The right answer depends on the size of the business, the risk appetite, and how much the owner wants to think about IT day to day.

## What to ask before you sign

If you are evaluating a provider, a short list of questions tends to separate serious operators from sales.

1. **What is included in the monthly fee, and what is extra?** Plain English list.
2. **What are your response time targets, and how do you measure them?**
3. **Can I see a sample monthly report?**
4. **How do you handle offboarding if we want to leave in 18 months?**
5. **Who is the person we actually talk to, and who covers them when they are off?**
6. **What does your security baseline include?**
7. **How do you handle major incidents?**

A provider who answers those cleanly is a safer bet than one who answers with slogans.

## Red flags

A few warning signs on the provider side:

- **Vague scope.** "Full IT support" without a written list of what that means.
- **Long lock-in with short notice periods.** 3-year contracts with 12-month notice are not consumer-friendly.
- **Per-ticket charging dressed up as managed.** If you pay a monthly fee and then also pay per issue, that is not managed.
- **No talk of security.** A managed provider who does not lead with MFA, backups, and patching is selling helpdesk, not IT.
- **Cannot produce a sample monthly report.** Reports are the receipt for the work.

Trust the red flags. A slightly cheaper provider with several of these is usually more expensive in the end.

## Signs you are ready to outsource

A short self-test.

- You have spent more than an hour this week on IT that is not strategic.
- You cannot remember the last backup restore test.
- A new starter takes more than a day to get set up properly.
- You have turned off security features "because they were causing tickets."
- You have had an incident and were not sure who to call first.
- Licence bills and software spend are not being reviewed.
- Key systems run on one person's knowledge.

If three or more of those are true, outsourcing some or all of the IT function is probably overdue.
