# When to upgrade business hardware — a practical SME checklist

Most small businesses — SMEs (small and medium enterprises) — I deal with in Ireland upgrade their hardware either too early or far too late. Too early usually means someone felt modern and replaced the whole office; too late is the norm, and it tends to look like a ten-year-old desktop wheezing through a Teams call while a member of staff quietly curses at it.

The honest answer is that neither "replace everything on a five-year cycle" nor "keep it until it dies" is the right policy. What you want is a short set of tests you can run against each class of equipment so upgrades happen at the point where they actually pay back.

## The real cost of old hardware

Before the checklist, a word on why this matters. Old hardware rarely kills a business dramatically. It drains it slowly. Three effects compound:

- **Time.** A laptop that takes four minutes to boot and another two to wake from sleep costs you ten to twenty minutes per person per day in dead time. Across a team of six over a year, that is real money.
- **Support cost.** Older devices generate more tickets — crashes, driver issues, battery swaps, odd noises. The bill climbs whether you are break/fix or managed.
- **Security.** A machine that cannot run Windows 11 sits unsupported as of late 2025, which means no security updates and a growing attack surface. Regulators and cyber insurers increasingly treat unsupported OSes (operating systems — Windows, macOS, and so on) as a material risk.

The test for an upgrade is not "does it still turn on?" It is "is it paying back what it costs me to keep it?"

## Laptops and desktops

The honest lifecycle for a business laptop is four to five years. For a desktop, five to seven. Beyond those windows the failure rate, support load, and productivity cost all climb fast. You can keep individual units going longer, but as a fleet policy those figures are where the cost curve turns.

Signs a specific machine has reached the end of its useful life:

- **Boot time over two minutes.** Everyone says their PC is slow; the actual number is the test. Two minutes from power-on to a working desktop is the line.
- **Cannot run Windows 11.** Windows 10 reached end of support in October 2025. Devices that cannot meet Windows 11's hardware requirements (TPM 2.0 — a small security chip on the motherboard — and a supported CPU, the computer's main processor) are on borrowed time. Microsoft sells extended security updates, but that is a stop-gap, not a plan.
- **Spinning hard disk, not SSD.** Replacing an HDD (older mechanical hard drive) with an SSD (solid-state drive — faster, no moving parts) is the single highest-return hardware upgrade still available. A five-year-old machine with an SSD can feel nearly new.
- **Battery holds less than an hour.** On a laptop, that is a sign the cells are tired. Often replaceable, sometimes not.
- **Fan runs constantly during normal use.** Either thermals are failing or the machine is being pushed to its limits by day-to-day work. Both mean the useful life is short.
- **More than one major issue per quarter.** Screen flicker, keyboard flake, port failure. One at a time is maintenance. Two or three is the machine telling you.

If you replace on a rolling basis rather than all at once, the budget stays flat and no single year is painful.

## Servers

The on-premises office server is less universal than it used to be, but plenty of Irish SMEs still have one — often for a specific line-of-business application, a file share, or local print and authentication.

Servers have a hard economic lifespan of about five years. After that, replacement parts get harder to find, warranties have long since expired, and the power draw starts to embarrass newer alternatives. The specific upgrade triggers:

- **Running Windows Server 2016 or older.** Those versions are approaching or past end of mainstream support. Security updates are ending.
- **No working warranty and no spare.** If a disk fails in your RAID and you cannot get a replacement within 24 hours, you are exposed.
- **The workload has outgrown it.** RAM (working memory) pinned, disk full, CPU saturated at month-end. An SSD and memory upgrade can buy time; eventually you need a new box.
- **You are paying to cool and power a machine that could be cloud.** If the server mostly runs a file share and a print queue, SharePoint and Microsoft 365 cover both for less money than the electricity.

A server replacement is a project, not a purchase. Plan it six months out, do the migration in a weekend with a tested rollback, and use the opportunity to review what really needs to be on-prem at all.

## Network — switches, firewalls, Wi-Fi access points

The least glamorous category and the one most businesses ignore the longest. Network hardware has a practical lifespan of five to seven years. The signs you are overdue:

- **Consumer-grade router for a business network.** A €60 router from a retailer is fine for home. In an office with a dozen devices, CCTV, Wi-Fi for staff and guests, and a VPN (virtual private network — a secure tunnel for remote staff), it is a bottleneck and a security risk.
- **Firewall firmware years out of date.** If your firewall vendor has issued critical updates and nothing has been applied, you have an unpatched edge device — the same class of target that appears in security news every few months.
- **Wi-Fi 4 or Wi-Fi 5 access points in a modern office.** Wi-Fi 6 and 6E access points are substantially more capable on dense networks. Older access points do not cope with today's device counts.
- **Cables and patch panel added to over the years, never tidied.** Cosmetic on the surface, but a sign nobody has looked at the network room for a while. Hidden surprises live in those cabinets.

## Peripherals — monitors, keyboards, headsets

Often overlooked because they are cheap individually. Two practical rules:

- **Monitors that flicker, have dead pixels, or are under 24 inches.** Replace with dual 24-inch or one 27-inch monitor per desk. The productivity lift from screen real estate is well-established and the outlay is small.
- **Headsets with broken boom mics.** A staff member on an online meeting with a bad mic represents a poor impression every time. Budget for decent USB headsets.

These are small line items that punch above their weight in day-to-day experience.

## A simple refresh plan

You do not need a capital refresh plan that looks like an enterprise IT strategy. For most Irish SMEs, this one page is enough:

1. **Keep a list of every business device** with its purchase date, user, and operating system.
2. **Replace anything over five years old** or that cannot run a supported OS, on a rolling basis.
3. **Budget for roughly one-fifth of the fleet per year.** Spreads cost, keeps the average age sensible.
4. **Review the list every quarter.** Five minutes with a coffee.

That is it. No elaborate depreciation schedule, no special software. A spreadsheet works.

## Buy, lease, or managed hardware?

There are three common models for Irish SMEs.

- **Outright purchase.** Simplest. Capital cost up front, full depreciation over 3-5 years.
- **Lease.** Monthly cost, fixed term, usually includes a refresh at end of term. Smoother cash flow.
- **Hardware-as-a-service (HaaS) / managed hardware.** Your IT provider supplies, configures, supports, and refreshes the device for a monthly fee.

Managed hardware tends to work well for SMEs that want predictable monthly costs and do not want to be the one ordering and commissioning new laptops every time someone starts. See the [managed hardware](/managed-hardware) page for how that looks in practice.

## A note on sustainability

Irish SMEs are increasingly asked about environmental impact in tenders and in client questionnaires. Refurbishing, reusing, and recycling old hardware well is both cheaper and a legitimate answer. Decent refurbished business laptops from reputable sources are usually half the price of new and fine for most users. At end of life, a WEEE-registered (Waste Electrical and Electronic Equipment — the Irish e-waste scheme) recycler is the right disposal route in Ireland and leaves an audit trail you can show.

## The shortest possible version

Replace things when they cost more to keep than to replace. Track ages in a simple list. Do it in batches, not all at once. Cover the unsexy categories — firewalls, switches, Wi-Fi — not just laptops.
