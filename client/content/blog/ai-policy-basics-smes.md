# AI policy basics for SMEs — a one-page template you can actually use

The best AI policy for a small business is the one staff will actually read and follow. That rules out twenty-page legal documents, obscure risk taxonomies, and copy-paste frameworks from enterprises with in-house compliance teams. What an Irish SME (small or medium-sized enterprise) needs is a one-page document that says clearly: here is what you can use, here is what you cannot, here is what to do if you are unsure.

This post walks through what a practical policy covers, with a worked template you can adapt. Fifteen minutes of work, and a set of rules that makes the rest of your AI adoption simpler.

## Why even have a policy

Three reasons, in order of immediacy.

**To keep sensitive data out of public AI tools.** Staff paste things into chatbots. Left to their own devices, some of those things will be client personal data, payroll records, or commercially sensitive information. A policy draws a line before that happens.

**To give staff permission to use AI well.** Without guidance, the conscientious employees worry they will get into trouble for using Copilot at all, and the enthusiastic ones try everything regardless. A policy sets the middle ground — here are the tools we support, here is how to use them.

**To have an answer when asked.** Clients, insurers, auditors, and tender processes increasingly ask what your AI policy is. A one-page document you can hand over is the right answer. "We don't have one" is the wrong one.

## What it should cover

Keep it to six short sections. Any more and people stop reading.

1. **Scope.** Who the policy applies to — all staff, contractors, interns. One sentence.
2. **Approved tools.** The specific tools the business has sanctioned, with a short note on what each is for.
3. **What data is allowed in AI tools.** What goes in, what never goes in, with examples.
4. **Output and responsibility.** Who is responsible for AI-generated work and how it should be reviewed before it leaves the business.
5. **Privacy and security.** MFA (multi-factor authentication — that second code on your phone when you log in), account hygiene, data retention settings, what to do if something goes wrong.
6. **Who to ask.** The named person or team staff should contact if they are unsure.

Six sections, one page. It should be readable in three minutes.

## A worked template

Use this as a starting point. Adapt the specifics to your business; do not copy it verbatim.

---

**[Your Firm] — AI Use Policy**

**1. Scope.** This policy applies to all staff, contractors, and anyone else using IT provided by or on behalf of [Your Firm].

**2. Approved AI tools.**

The following tools are approved for use on business tasks:

- **Microsoft 365 Copilot** (where licensed) — drafting emails, summarising Teams meetings, Excel analysis, document search.
- **[Approved general assistant, e.g. ChatGPT Team]** — brainstorming, rewriting, summarising public documents.
- **[Approved writing assistant]** — grammar and style checks.
- **[Other sanctioned tools your business uses]**.

Staff should not use personal or free AI accounts for business tasks without prior approval.

**3. What data is allowed in AI tools.**

**Allowed:** public information, internal non-sensitive text, brainstorming, drafts that do not contain client or personal data.

**Not allowed in any AI tool — approved or otherwise:** client personal data, payroll data, financial statements before publication, passwords or security credentials, board papers, anything marked confidential, anything subject to a non-disclosure agreement.

If you are unsure whether something is allowed, treat it as not allowed and ask first.

**4. Output and responsibility.**

The member of staff who uses AI to produce work is responsible for the output. All AI-generated content — emails, documents, code, images — must be reviewed by the staff member before it is sent, published, or shared externally.

Specifically: AI can generate suggestions. A human decides what goes out under our name.

**5. Privacy and security.**

- MFA is required on every account that accesses an approved AI tool.
- Data retention and training settings must be configured correctly before first use (IT provides a short guide).
- Any AI account should be created on a business email address, not a personal one.
- If you think sensitive data was entered into an AI tool by mistake, tell [named person] the same day.

**6. Questions.**

If you are unsure about any of this, ask [named person / team] before acting. Asking is always fine; no question is too small.

**Policy owner:** [name]. **Last reviewed:** [date]. **Next review:** [date + 6 months].

---

That is it. One page, six sections, specific to your business once you fill in the blanks.

## Things that usually get over-engineered

If you look at enterprise AI policies, you will see talk of algorithmic risk categories, model impact assessments, and AI ethics committees. Most of this is not appropriate for a small business. It tends to get copy-pasted into documents nobody follows.

At SME scale, skip the heavy frameworks. You are not training your own models. You are using vendor tools. What matters is the five practical controls above. If and when you grow into something that needs more, you will know.

## The EU AI Act — a word of reassurance

The EU AI Act has been in the news. Its risk-based obligations mostly apply to providers of high-risk AI systems and to "general-purpose AI models" above thresholds that small businesses will not hit. Routine SME use of Microsoft Copilot or ChatGPT is well below any of these bars.

That said, obligations on users of AI systems do exist — around transparency, human oversight, and certain restricted practices. For most Irish SMEs, the policy above is already aligned with what the Act would ask for. The exception is any business deploying AI in regulated categories (HR screening, biometric, critical infrastructure) — those sectors need a specific assessment.

## GDPR is the one to get right

For Irish SMEs, the rule that matters most in practice is still GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation — the EU data protection law). If you put personal data into an AI tool that processes or retains it, you have a data processor relationship that needs a DPA (data processing agreement — the contract required under GDPR when a third party handles personal data for you), a lawful basis, and a record in your processing register.

The practical application: your approved tools should all be ones you have a DPA with. Free, personal-account chatbots do not meet that standard. This is why the policy draws the line the way it does — it is not arbitrary, it is GDPR-shaped.

## Rolling it out

A short policy is only useful if staff know about it. Rollout for a small business is simple:

1. **Send the policy by email** with a short cover message — two minutes to read, please reply to acknowledge.
2. **Run a 20-minute session** walking through it, with live examples.
3. **Add it to onboarding** so every new starter sees it on day one.
4. **Review twice a year.** Tools change. Risks change. Keep the policy current.

No training platform, no LMS, no compliance software needed. If you grow to the point where those tools make sense, you will also have grown past the one-page policy.
